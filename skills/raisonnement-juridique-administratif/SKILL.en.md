# Administrative legal reasoning

French sources are quoted in free translation; the French text prevails.

This skill guides the analysis of a case file and the drafting of a decision before the French administrative courts.

## Scope, and what must not be done

This skill applies to the administrative order of courts: the Council of State (*Conseil d'État*), the administrative courts of appeal (*cours administratives d'appel*), the administrative tribunals (*tribunaux administratifs*), the specialised administrative courts.

It does not transpose to litigation before the ordinary (judicial) courts, and the converse is true. The Code of Civil Procedure does not govern the office of the administrative judge (*office du juge*): never base an administrative statement of grounds on articles 4, 6, 7, 9, 12 or 16 of the Code of Civil Procedure, nor on the structure of the civil judgment under articles 454 and 480 of the same code. Administrative contentious procedure is inquisitorial, the investigation of the case is directed by the judge, and the decision follows its own rules of form.

## Mandatory rule on sources

Never cite a text, a decision or a paragraph of the grounds without having retrieved its full text. A truncated excerpt taken from a search result is not sufficient for a legal citation. This rule applies to articles of codes as well as to the grounds of decisions.

Never cite from memory a case number, a date or a judicial formation. If the exact reference has not been found in a corpus, say so.

A source is designated by its exact name, as identified by the search engine, never by a reconstructed title.

## Order in which sources are consulted

1. The applicable text, in the version in force at the relevant date, and not in its current version when the dispute concerns an earlier decision.
2. The case law of the Council of State, starting with the most solemn formations and the decisions published in the Reports (Recueil Lebon).
3. Where relevant, the Constitutional Council (*Conseil constitutionnel*), the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU), the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR), depending on the norm invoked.
4. Legal scholarship last, and always identified as such.

Never confine oneself to the recent decisions of a single chamber without having checked whether a decision of the Assembly or of the Section exists on the question.

## 1. Taking up the case file

1. Identify the parties and their capacity, the author of the contested act and the defendant administration.
2. Identify the nature of the action: excess of power, full jurisdiction (*plein contentieux*), interim proceedings (*référé*), and where relevant the applicable special litigation regime.
3. Identify the contested act precisely: nature, author, date, scope. An error in identifying the act vitiates the entire reasoning.
4. Reconstruct the chronology: facts, decision, any prior administrative appeal, referral to the court, time limits.
5. List the exhibits and the written pleadings, distinguishing what is produced from what is merely alleged.
6. Check at the outset the jurisdiction of the court, admissibility and the existence of a dispute to be decided.

## 2. Claims, pleas, arguments

| Element | Definition | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Claims (*conclusions*) | What is asked of the judge: annulment, compensation, injunction, costs | Rule on them in the operative part, omitting nothing and not exceeding what is asked |
| Plea (*moyen*) | Reason of law or of fact invoked in support of the claims | Answer it in the grounds, subject to the economy of pleas |
| Argument | Element of discussion with no legal effect of its own | Do not answer it |

The judge rules on all the claims and only on them. Ruling beyond the claims, or omitting to rule on one of them, vitiates the decision.

Pleas attach to distinct legal causes of action, external legality and internal legality. A plea falling under a new legal cause of action, raised after the expiry of the time limit for bringing the action, is inadmissible, subject to pleas of public policy. This is the rule stemming from the Intercopie case law: retrieve its exact reference and verbatim text from the corpus before citing it.

A fourth category, distinct from the three preceding ones, must be isolated: the **preliminary questions**. They are dealt with before the merits and are not in the nature of pleas.

- No need to adjudicate (*non-lieu à statuer*), when the claims have become devoid of purpose, in particular through a tax relief granted during the proceedings or the repeal of the contested act.
- Discontinuance.
- Time bar and lateness.
- Objections of inadmissibility (*fins de non-recevoir*) raised by the defendant: inadmissibility of new claims on appeal, lack of standing, absence of the mandatory prior administrative appeal, absence of a prior complaint in tax matters.

Classifying an objection of inadmissibility among the applicant's pleas, or a question of admissibility among the procedural pleas, distorts the plan of the decision.

See `references/conclusions-moyens-arguments.md`.

## 3. The grid specific to each type of litigation

The plan of the grounds is not the same depending on the nature of the dispute. Choose the grid before characterising the pleas: a poorly chosen grid produces false characterisations.

### Excess of power and objective full-jurisdiction proceedings

Two legal causes of action, in this order: **external legality**, which comprises lack of competence, procedural defect and defect of form, then **internal legality**, which comprises lack of legal basis, error of law, error of fact, error in legal characterisation, manifest error of assessment and misuse of power.

Pleas of external legality are the most often neglected: look for them expressly.

### Full-jurisdiction tax proceedings

Do not use the categories of external legality and internal legality here: they are not those of the subject. Three stages, in this order: the **regularity of the judgment** when the contested act is a judgment; the **tax assessment procedure**, which concerns only the regularity of the audit and of the adjustment; the **merits** of the tax assessment, tax base, rate, characterisation, penalties.

**Characteristic trap.** The admissibility of the prior complaint (existence, time limit, proof of dispatch) is **not** a plea relating to the tax assessment procedure: it is a preliminary question, whether raised by the applicant or objected by the administration. Classifying it under procedure leads to answering in the wrong place, and sometimes to ruling on the merits of an inadmissible application.

Tax litigation moreover has its own rules, burden of proof, taxpayer safeguards, tax relief and no need to adjudicate, substitution of legal basis, set-off, penalties, which call for a separate development: see `references/contentieux-fiscal.md`.

### Full-jurisdiction liability proceedings

The points of liability, in this order: the **generating event**, fault or no-fault liability depending on the applicable regime; the **causal link**, direct and certain; then **each head of damage** invoked, with its assessment. Check along the way the prior claim for compensation and the four-year limitation period.

### Appeal

The **regularity of the judgment** comes first, before the soundness of the solution adopted. Do not confuse the two: criticism of the statement of grounds is a plea of regularity, criticism of the solution is a plea on the merits.

Identify the roles with care. The **appellant** is the party who brings the case before the court and asks for the annulment or the variation of the judgment, **even when it is the administration**: its claims are those of the claimant in the appeal proceedings. The **respondent** (*intimé*) asks for the dismissal of the application and the upholding of the judgment. Placing the annulment of the judgment on the defendant's side is an inversion that makes everything that follows incoherent.

The mere request for annulment or variation of the judgment is a **claim**, not a plea: do not list it among the pleas.

Finally, check the inadmissibility of new claims on appeal, and the devolutive effect.

### Cassation

Two sets: the **regularity of the contested decision**, composition of the formation, statement of grounds, procedure; and its **soundness**, error of law, distortion of the exhibits or of the facts, error in legal characterisation.

The cassation judge does not retry the facts: a plea that merely invites their reassessment is ineffective, save for distortion. Distinguish what falls under review of legal characterisation from what falls under the sovereign assessment of the trial judges.

## 4. Office of the administrative judge

Structuring points, developed in `references/office-du-juge-administratif.md`:

- The statement of grounds is mandatory, article L. 9 of the Code of Administrative Justice.
- The procedure is inquisitorial: the judge directs the investigation, orders useful measures and may request the production of exhibits.
- The judge raises pleas of public policy of his own motion. He first informs the parties and invites them to submit their observations, article R. 611-7 of the Code of Administrative Justice. Omitting this communication is a ground for annulment.
- The judge ruling on excess of power may confine himself to upholding one plea sufficient to pronounce the annulment. This economy of pleas has limits, in particular when the examination of another plea governs the measures of execution or the injunction sought. Decide this point explicitly rather than by default.
- The judge in full-jurisdiction proceedings rules on the situation at the date of his decision, the judge ruling on excess of power assesses legality in principle at the date of the act. Check the rule specific to the litigation at issue before fixing the date of assessment.

## 5. Syllogism and statement of grounds

The grounds are written in direct style, in numbered paragraphs, in the present indicative. The "considérant que" formula ("whereas") is abandoned.

**Major premise.** State the applicable rule, text and its interpretation in the case law, in the version applicable to the dispute, specifying the conditions of application and the rules of evidence.

**Minor premise.** Apply it to the facts established by the investigation, assess the exhibits, answer the pleas, characterise legally.

**Conclusion.** Draw the consequence, consistently with the major and minor premises, and carry it into the operative part.

Proscribe hypothetical, dubitative or superfluous grounds. A ground that supports no conclusion has no place in the decision.

See `references/syllogisme-et-motivation.md`.

## 6. Authority of decisions

Two scales combine, the judicial formation and the level of publication. They are not substitutes for one another: a published Assembly decision prevails, an unreported decision of a chamber does not on its own found a solution of principle.

Formations, in decreasing order of authority: Judicial Assembly (*Assemblée du contentieux*), Judicial Section (*Section du contentieux*), combined chambers, chamber sitting alone, single judge and orders.

Publication: published in the Reports (Recueil Lebon), mentioned in the Tables, unreported. In internal corpora, these levels correspond to the letters A, B and C.

Opinions delivered at the request of a court, article L. 113-1 of the Code of Administrative Justice, have an authority of their own which must be flagged as such.

Systematically check that an old decision has not been abandoned. A case abstract (*fiche*) recording the abandonment of a line of case law prevails over the old solution.

See `references/hierarchie-jurisprudence-administrative.md`.

## 7. Structure of the decision

Recitals (*visas*), grounds, operative part. The mandatory particulars are set out in article R. 741-2 of the Code of Administrative Justice: retrieve the text in force before checking a decision.

See `references/structure-decision.md`.

## 8. Points of vigilance

To do:

- Check jurisdiction, admissibility and the existence of a dispute before the merits.
- Choose the grid of the litigation at issue before characterising the pleas.
- Deal with external legality before internal legality, unless there is an express reason to do otherwise.
- Deal with preliminary questions, including objections of inadmissibility, before the merits.
- Communicate any plea raised of the court's own motion before relying on it.
- Retrieve the full text of every article and every ground cited.
- Indicate the level of publication of every decision cited.
- Check the version of the text applicable at the relevant date.

To avoid:

- Transposing a rule of civil procedure.
- Founding a solution of principle on an unreported decision.
- Citing a text in its current version for a dispute governed by an earlier version.
- Writing a hypothetical or dubitative ground.
- Answering the arguments rather than the pleas.
- Using the categories of external and internal legality in tax matters.
- Classifying the admissibility of the prior complaint among the pleas relating to the tax assessment procedure.
- Placing the annulment of the judgment on the respondent's side, or treating that request as a plea.
- Naming an exhibit or a source otherwise than by its real name.
