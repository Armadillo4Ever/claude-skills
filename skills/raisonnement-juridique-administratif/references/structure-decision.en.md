# Structure of the decision

French sources are quoted in free translation; the French text prevails.

## The three parts

**Recitals (*visas*).** They identify the procedure and the texts. They comprise the account of the procedure followed, the written pleadings and their dates, the mention of the other documents in the file, then the texts referred to, the Constitution and international commitments where relevant, the applicable codes and Acts, the Code of Administrative Justice.

**Grounds (*motifs*).** Numbered paragraphs, direct style. They follow the order of examination adopted and carry the syllogism.

**Operative part (*dispositif*).** Introduced by "Decides:" (*Décide :*), it lists numbered articles. The last article provides for notification.

The mandatory particulars of the decision are laid down by article R. 741-2 of the Code of Administrative Justice.
Link: https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000033340950

Delivery is governed by article R. 741-1 of the same code: "Save where it is read out in open court, the decision is delivered by being made available at the registry of the court."
Link: https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000042535284

## Requirements for the operative part

- One head of claim, one article, or an express dismissal.
- The operative part must be executable without having to interpret the grounds.
- An injunction specifies what must be done and within what time, and where relevant the periodic penalty payment.
- Costs are the subject of a separate article.
- The notification article designates the parties by their exact names, as well as, where appropriate, the minister concerned.

## Consistency between the three parts

Three mechanical checks, to be made systematically:

1. every text cited in the grounds appears in the recitals;
2. every head of claim set out in the recitals receives an answer in the grounds and in the operative part;
3. no article of the operative part rests on a missing ground.
