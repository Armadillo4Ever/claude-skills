# Office of the administrative judge

French sources are quoted in free translation; the French text prevails.

## Statement of grounds

The obligation to state the grounds of judgments is laid down by article L. 9 of the Code of Administrative Justice.
Link: https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006449163/2021-06-13

The statement of grounds must be complete, that is, answer all the pleas (*moyens*) the judge is bound to answer; faithful, restating exactly what was argued; impartial, refraining from any value judgment on persons; and precise, referring to the exhibits by their nature, date and author.

## Inquisitorial procedure

The investigation of the case is directed by the judge, not by the parties. Several practical consequences follow:

- the judge sets the timetable, orders communications and may close the investigation;
- he may request from the administration the production of exhibits it alone holds, and draw the consequences of a refusal to produce;
- the administration bears in practice the burden of establishing what it alone can establish, the burden of proof being adjusted according to the type of litigation;
- the judge may order a measure of investigation, an expert report, a site visit, an inquiry.

Do not transpose the civil regime of the burden of proof. Check the regime specific to the litigation at issue.

## Pleas of public policy

The judge is bound to raise pleas of public policy of his own motion. He may not do so without having informed the parties and having given them the opportunity to submit their observations: article R. 611-7 of the Code of Administrative Justice.
Link: https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000038114788

No prior communication, irregular decision. This is one of the defects most frequently sanctioned in cassation.

Classically falling within this category, subject to verification in the particular case and to developments in the case law: lack of jurisdiction of the court seised, lack of competence of the author of the act, disregard of the scope of application of the statute, inadmissibility of the application, res judicata, no need to adjudicate.

Do not rely on a memorised list. Before raising a plea of the court's own motion, check in the case law corpus that it is indeed a plea of public policy in the litigation considered, and retrieve the decision that says so.

## Economy of pleas

The judge ruling on excess of power may confine himself to upholding one plea sufficient to pronounce the annulment, without examining the others. This is not a drafting convenience, it is a choice with effects:

- it determines what the administration will have to do to take its decision again lawfully;
- it governs the scope of any injunction sought;
- it may leave uncertainty on questions that will arise again.

Where claims for an injunction are made, or where the examination of another plea sheds light on execution, examine that additional plea and say so.

Decide this point explicitly in the draft, and not by omission.

## Limits of the office

- Rule on all the claims (*conclusions*) and only on them. Failure to rule and ruling beyond what is asked both vitiate the decision.
- Do not distort the parties' written submissions.
- Respect the adversarial nature of the procedure, including for exhibits produced late and for pleas raised of the court's own motion.
- Do not rule on the expediency of the act where the review is a review of legality.

## Date of assessment

- Excess of power: legality is assessed in principle at the date of the contested act.
- Full-jurisdiction proceedings (*plein contentieux*): the judge rules in principle in the light of the situation existing at the date of his decision.

These principles have numerous exceptions depending on the type of litigation. Check the applicable rule before fixing the date of assessment, and state it in the grounds where it is not obvious.
