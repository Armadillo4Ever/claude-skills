# Authority of decisions of the administrative courts

French sources are quoted in free translation; the French text prevails.

Two scales combine: the judicial formation and the level of publication. Neither is sufficient on its own.

## Judicial formations of the Council of State

In decreasing order of authority:

| Formation | What it signals |
|---|---|
| Judicial Assembly (*Assemblée du contentieux*) | Question of principle, reversal of case law, questions of the first importance |
| Judicial Section (*Section du contentieux*) | Question of principle of notable importance, clarification of a line of case law |
| Combined chambers | Sensitive or new question, within the field of several chambers |
| Chamber sitting alone | Application of established case law |
| Single judge, orders | Cases presenting no difficulty, or dismissal without investigation |

Decisions of the interim relief judge (*juge des référés*) follow a logic of their own, that of urgency and of the particular office of the interim relief judge. Do not give them the scope of a decision on the merits.

## Levels of publication

| Level | Scope | Usual internal notation |
|---|---|---|
| Published in the Reports (Recueil Lebon) | Decision of principle | A |
| Mentioned in the Tables of the Reports | Identified contribution to the case law, more limited | B |
| Unreported | Application of a settled solution | C |

An unreported decision does not on its own found a solution of principle. It may illustrate, it does not establish.

## Rules of articulation

- An Assembly decision prevails over any decision of a lower formation, including a more recent one, as long as it has not been abandoned.
- A decision published in the Reports prevails over a decision mentioned in the Tables, which prevails over an unreported decision.
- Between two decisions of the same level, the more recent prevails.
- An abandonment of a line of case law, signalled by the case abstract (*fichage*), prevails over the old solution whatever its authority may have been.

## Contentious opinions

Opinions delivered at the request of a court pursuant to article L. 113-1 of the Code of Administrative Justice decide a new question of law presenting a serious difficulty and arising in numerous disputes. They have an authority of their own, distinct from that of a judicial decision. Flag them as such and do not present them as judgments.

## Case abstracts

The abstracting (*fichage*) of a decision, its abstracts and the Tables, indicates what the decision has actually established. A major premise repeated from one decision to another is established; what appears only in the minor premise of a particular case is not necessarily so.

Check the case abstract before deriving a general rule from a decision.

## Check before citation

1. Retrieve the full text of the decision, not only its summary.
2. Note the formation and the level of publication, and indicate them.
3. Check that the decision has not been abandoned or qualified.
4. Cite the relevant paragraph verbatim, with its number.
