# Tax litigation: specific rules

French sources are quoted in free translation; the French text prevails.

Supplement to the skill `raisonnement-juridique-administratif`. Litigation on the tax base falls under full-jurisdiction proceedings (*plein contentieux*), but it is governed by a procedural code of its own, the Book of Tax Procedures (*livre des procédures fiscales*, LPF), and by rules of evidence, admissibility and sanction that cannot be inferred from the ordinary law of administrative litigation. The articles cited below were verified on Légifrance on 9 September 2026, in the version in force at that date; check the version applicable to the dispute before citing.

## 1. The prerequisite: the complaint

Tax litigation begins before the administration, not before the judge. Complaints seeking "either the correction of errors made in the base or the calculation of tax assessments, or the benefit of a right resulting from a legislative or regulatory provision" fall within contentious jurisdiction ([LPF, art. L. 190](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000045917153)).

**Time limit for the complaint.** For taxes other than local direct taxes, the complaint must be submitted "no later than 31 December of the second year following that" of the entry in the collection roll, of the payment of the contested tax or of the occurrence of the event giving rise to the complaint ([LPF, art. R.* 196-1](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000054553358), version in force since 30 July 2026: check the applicable version, the article has been amended).

**Bringing the case before the tribunal.** Decisions taken on complaints "may be brought before the administrative tribunal" in matters of direct taxes and turnover taxes ([LPF, art. L. 199](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000051200782)). The action must be brought "within two months from the day of receipt of the notice by which the administration notifies the taxpayer of the decision taken on the complaint"; the taxpayer who has received no decision within six months may bring the case before the tribunal as from the expiry of that period ([LPF, art. R.* 199-1](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006316636)).

**Consequence for the plan of the decision.** The existence, date and regularity of the complaint are **preliminary questions of admissibility**, never pleas relating to the tax assessment procedure. An application made without a prior complaint, or after the expiry of the time limit, is inadmissible, and the judge does not examine the merits.

## 2. The tax assessment procedure

It concerns only the regularity of the audit and of the adjustment. The safeguards most often invoked:

- **The notice of audit.** A contradictory examination of the personal tax situation, an audit or an examination of accounts "may not be undertaken without the taxpayer having been informed by the sending or delivery of a notice", which states "expressly, on pain of nullity of the procedure, that the taxpayer has the option of being assisted by an adviser" ([LPF, art. L. 47](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000033815149)).
- **The statement of reasons for the proposed adjustment.** It "must be reasoned in such a way as to enable him to submit his observations or to make his acceptance known" ([LPF, art. L. 57](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000022177777)).
- **Ex officio assessment.** The bases "are brought to the taxpayer's knowledge at least thirty days before the entry of the tax assessments in the collection roll" ([LPF, art. L. 76](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000033817294)).
- **The right of reassessment.** For income tax and corporation tax, it "is exercised until the end of the third year following that in respect of which the tax is due", with extended periods, in particular in the case of undisclosed activity ([LPF, art. L. 169](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000054358875)).

**The sanction of irregularities.** A **non-substantial** procedural error allows the judge to pronounce, "on that ground alone, [only] the discharge of the surcharges and fines, to the exclusion of the duties owed in principal and of the interest for late payment"; the discharge of the whole is pronounced only "where the error has had the effect of infringing the rights of the defence or where it is one of those for which nullity is expressly provided by law" ([LPF, art. L. 80 CA](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006315091)). Characterising the irregularity as substantial or not is therefore the first question, before drawing the consequence.

## 3. The burden of proof

It is not inferred from the capacity of claimant: it depends on the procedure followed.

- Where the commission for direct taxes or the abuse of law committee has been consulted, "the administration bears the burden of proof in the event of a complaint, whatever the opinion delivered"; however "the burden of proof lies with the taxpayer where the accounts contain serious irregularities and the tax assessment has been established in accordance with the opinion" ([LPF, art. L. 192](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000037993633)).
- The taxpayer who has **accepted** the adjustment or has **refrained from replying** within the time limit, and who subsequently makes a complaint, "may obtain the discharge or reduction of the tax assessment by demonstrating that it is excessive": the burden of proof lies with him ([LPF, art. R.* 194-1](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006316300)).

In the major premise, always state who bears the burden of proof and why, before assessing the exhibits.

## 4. The merits

Tax base, rate, chargeability, characterisation of income or transactions, applicable regime, allowances and reductions. This is where the interpretation of the tax law and the characterisation of the facts are discussed; procedure no longer has a place here.

**Set-off.** Where a taxpayer requests the discharge or reduction of a tax assessment, "the administration may, at any stage of the procedure and notwithstanding the expiry of the limitation periods, effect or request a set-off within the limit of the contested tax assessment" between the justified reliefs and the shortfalls found ([LPF, art. L. 203](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006315462)); the set-off operates "under the same conditions for the benefit of the taxpayer" in the event of over-taxation or double taxation ([LPF, art. L. 205](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006315470)).

**Substitution of legal basis.** The administration may, during the proceedings, request that the tax assessment be maintained on another legal basis, provided that the taxpayer is not deprived of any of the safeguards attached to the new basis. The conditions are set by the case law: retrieve its exact reference and verbatim text from the corpus before citing it.

## 5. Sanctions

Distinguish three things, each with its own regime and its own statement of reasons:

- **Interest for late payment**, which is not a sanction: "any claim of a tax nature [...] which has not been paid within the statutory period gives rise to the payment of interest for late payment" ([CGI, art. 1727](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000051213330)).
- **Surcharges for failure to file or late filing of a return** ([CGI, art. 1728](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000053546784)).
- **Surcharges for an insufficient return**: 40% "in the event of deliberate breach", 80% "in the event of abuse of law" or fraudulent manoeuvres ([CGI, art. 1729](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000020051904)).

Penalties are sanctions: their statement of reasons, the proof of deliberate breach, which lies with the administration, and the principle of proportionality call for an examination distinct from that of the duties.

## 6. Preliminary questions specific to the subject

- **Tax relief granted during the proceedings** renders the claims devoid of purpose to that extent: no need to adjudicate, to be pronounced before any examination on the merits.
- **The prior complaint** (section 1): existence, time limit, proof of dispatch, identity between the tax assessment contested before the administration and before the judge.
- **New claims**: the taxpayer may not ask the judge for more than he asked the administration, nor contest a tax assessment that his complaint did not cover.

## What this file does not cover

Litigation on tax collection, distinct from litigation on the tax base and subject to its own rules of allocation between the two orders of courts; local direct taxes, whose complaint time limits differ; special procedures (abuse of law, flagrancy, searches of premises). For each of these, start again from the LPF in its applicable version.
