# The three-column table

French sources are quoted in free translation; the French text prevails.

## What this table is, and what it is not

The three-column table is the reading instrument for an amending text. It places side by side, for each provision affected, the law as it is, what the draft does to it, and the law as it will be. It serves the rapporteur, the section, the assembly, the ministry, Parliament: each reads at a glance what disappears, what appears and what remains.

The table **records**. It proposes no correction, reports no infelicity of drafting, does not judge the expediency of an amendment: those observations belong to the legislative drafting analysis and to the review on the merits, which draw on the table but do not appear in it. A table that comments is no longer a table.

Nor does it replace the draft: the draft remains the text, the table is only a reading of it. Any discrepancy between the two is resolved in favour of the draft.

## The three columns, in this order

1. **The law in force.** The amended text, in its version applicable at the date of the review, cited by its exact title and verified on Légifrance at that date. The entire provision affected is reproduced there (the article, or the subdivision when the article is long and the amendment bears only on that subdivision), in paragraphs (*alinéas*), as Légifrance carries them. When the draft institutes a provision that does not exist, the column bears only the mention "article created" (or "division created"), never a guessed text.
2. **The draft text.** The instruction or the provision, reproduced in full and as it stands, with its label ("3°", "b)", "I."), without rewording, without summary, without ellipsis points. This is the column of proof: the reader must be able to verify column 3 from this column alone.
3. **The consolidated text.** What the law in force will become if the draft is adopted, in visible correction: deleted passages struck through, added passages marked (bold or underlined, according to the convention announced at the head of the table). This column is **always filled**. For a self-standing provision, which amends no text, it reproduces the provision, since that provision will be the law. For a repeal, it bears the repealed text struck through in full, and not the mention "repealed" alone.

A legend at the head of the table states the convention adopted (strikethrough, bold, underline) and the date at which the law in force was taken.

## The cardinal principle: designate, do not copy

The most frequent error, and the hardest to see, arises from copying. When a drafter, human or machine, is asked to copy the text of the draft and the articles in force, he abbreviates, rewords, cuts a sentence in the middle, pastes an insertion at the end of the article instead of placing it where the instruction places it, forgets to strike through. Seven defects of this kind were found on a single file.

The remedy is one of principle: **the text is never copied, it is taken from its source, and only the operation is described.**

- The text of the draft comes from the draft document, paragraph by paragraph.
- The law in force comes from Légifrance, paragraph by paragraph, in the dated version.
- The consolidation is a mechanical operation applied to these two materials: one designates the target (which article, which subdivision, which paragraph, which words, which occurrence), the operation (replace, insert after, supplement, repeal, delete, re-enact, renumber) and the object (the article, the paragraph, the sentence, the words), then one applies.

What is not copied can be neither truncated nor abbreviated. For short fragments only (words replaced, words inserted, reference deleted), the words are quoted in quotation marks, exactly.

## Distinguishing the anchor from the target

In "After article L. 186-2, articles L. 186-2-1 and L. 186-2-2 are inserted", article L. 186-2 is not amended: it is the anchor point. The articles created are L. 186-2-1 and L. 186-2-2, and it is these that the row of the table bears, with "article created" in column 1. Showing L. 186-2 as an amended text is an error that suggests an amendment that does not exist.

Likewise, "Article 7 is amended as follows: 1° ... 2° ..." opens several operations on a single target: as many rows, or a single row bearing the whole consolidation of the article, but never a row that would account only for the 1°.

## One row per operation of the draft

The division follows the draft, not the amended code. An operation that inserts a chapter made up of two articles, or two consecutive articles, gives a single row: those articles proceed from the same movement of the text and are read together. Conversely, two distinct operations on the same article in force (a replacement of words in the first paragraph, a sentence added to the third) may fit on one row if the consolidation shows both; otherwise, two rows, in the order of the draft.

The rows follow one another in the order of the articles of the draft, then of their subdivisions. They are not reordered according to the amended code: the reader follows the draft.

## The cases that trip people up

- **Tables of applicability in the overseas territories** are amended row by row ("the eighth row of the table in section I is replaced by..."). A table row is not a paragraph; confusing them makes the amendment bear on an entirely different place in the article. Column 3 then reproduces the table with the row struck through and the new row.
- **"Replace words" and "insert after words"** are not to be confused: "the words X are replaced by Y" makes X disappear; "after the words X, the words Y are inserted" keeps X. The first is rendered as X struck through then Y; the second, X then Y marked.
- **The occurrence**: "the second occurrence of the words" does not target the first. The consolidation applies to the one the instruction designates.
- **"The remainder unchanged"** (*le reste sans changement*) bounds the replacement to the beginning quoted; the rest of the paragraph is reproduced as it stands, without marking.
- **A range** ("articles 20-12 to 20-14") covers the intermediate articles.
- **An article created by the draft itself** and amended by a later article of the same draft: the law in force of the second row is the text as it results from the first, and the legend says so.
- **A provision repealed then re-enacted**: the row of the re-enactment bears "article re-enacted" in column 1, with the reference of the repeal.
- **Transitional provisions, provisions on entry into force and on application in the overseas territories** are self-standing: their column 3 reproduces the text, they are not omitted on the ground that they amend nothing.

## What cannot be done must be said

An instruction whose target could not be identified (article not found in the dated version, ambiguous designation, paragraph whose rank corresponds to nothing) is neither applied by guesswork nor passed over in silence. Column 3 then bears, in place of the consolidation, the mention of the incident: "target not identified: article L. 512-16 has only four paragraphs, the instruction targets the fifth". The reader knows what he must check himself.

Likewise, a Légifrance version whose date does not match that of the review (text amended in the meantime, deferred version) is reported in column 1.

## Checks before delivering

- Each row has its three columns filled, column 3 without exception.
- The text of the draft in column 2 is complete and consistent with the document, label included.
- Each article cited in column 1 has been verified on Légifrance in its version applicable at the date of the review, and that date appears in the legend.
- Created articles bear "article created", anchor points do not appear as amended texts.
- The consolidation has been reread in full: a replacement of words has affected only the designated portion, an insertion is in its place, an added sentence remains in its paragraph, nothing is pasted at the end of the article, what disappears is struck through.
- Inapplicable instructions are reported as such, with the reason.
- The table contains no comment, no proposed correction, no assessment.

## Articulation with the other skills

The reading of instructions (verb, target, object, counting of paragraphs, compound designations) is a matter for the skill `legistique-modifications-de-textes`, which this skill assumes to be acquired. The form of the draft is a matter for `relecture-legistique`, its substance for `examen-consultatif-projet-de-texte`. The table is produced after the reading of the instructions and before the review on the merits, of which it is the basis.
