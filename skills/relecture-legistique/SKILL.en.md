# Legislative proofreading of a text

French sources are quoted in free translation; the French text prevails.

This skill puts a text through the sieve of the rules of form of the Legislative Drafting Guide (*Guide de légistique*), published on Légifrance by the General Secretariat of the Government and the Council of State (*Conseil d'État*). It rules neither on expediency, nor on legality, nor on the solution adopted: for the legal review of a draft, see the advisory review skill; to read and apply an amending instruction, see the skill on the legislative drafting of amendments.

## The method

Four rules, without exception:

1. **Report only the deviations.** A check that lists what is right drowns what is wrong.
2. **Quote the faulty passage** in quotation marks, briefly, so that the drafter can find it.
3. **Ground each deviation in an entry (*fiche*) of the Guide**, named by its number. A deviation without a source cannot be verified, and is therefore unusable. What rests only on usage is classified "to be checked", not "deviation".
4. **Propose the compliant wording**, ready to be taken up, and not a mere description of the problem.

The references below refer to the entries of the Guide, whose numbering is stable from one edition to the next; the pages cited are those of the 3<sup>rd</sup> edition (2017). A more recent edition may have moved a page, not an entry: check on Légifrance before citing a page.

## The grid

### 1. Language (fiche 3.3.1)

- No foreign or Latin term where a French equivalent exists: "in fine", "in situ", "a contrario", "ex nihilo", "de jure" are to be translated. Accepted exceptions: "a priori", "a posteriori", "quantum", "référendum", "alinéa".
- No anglicisms: "impacter" (affecter), "générer" (engendrer), "présumer" in the sense of supposer, minister "en charge de" (chargé de).
- Simple sentences: no nested subordinate clauses, no double negatives, no piling up of nouns. "Assurer la réalisation" is written "réaliser".
- "Et/ou" is replaced by "ou"; "le ou les" is proscribed.
- No inclusive writing (circular of the Prime Minister of 21 November 2017).
- Catch-all terms to be avoided (fiche 3.3.2): "concerner" (intéressé, en cause, relatif à), "effectuer", "décliner" a rule; unnecessary adverbs ("rigoureusement interdit"); "au niveau de" (dans); "ledit", "ladite" (ce, cette); the compounds in "sus-" ("susdit", "susmentionné"), replaced by "mentionné ci-dessus". "Susvisé" remains accepted, on its conditions (block 8).

### 2. Tense and mood (fiche 3.3.1)

- The present indicative, which has imperative force, and not the future: "il est procédé", not "il sera procédé".
- No "doit" to reinforce an obligation: "les fédérations transmettent", not "doivent transmettre".
- A provision inserted in a code is timeless: "la commission est chargée de", not "il est créé une commission chargée de".

### 3. Acronyms, abbreviations, parentheses (fiche 3.3.1)

- In an Act or a decree: no abbreviations, no words in parentheses, no footnotes.
- No acronyms in the enacting terms; "la Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés", not "la CNIL". An acronym is accepted only in an explanatory memorandum or a notice, spelled out at its first occurrence.
- The letter of code article numbers is followed by a full stop ("article L. 121-1", "R. 3211-2"), except LO ("LO 44") (typographic charter, p. 699).

### 4. Legal terms (fiche 3.3.2)

Each term in its precise meaning:

- "sous l'autorité" (hierarchical power) is not "sous la tutelle" or "la surveillance";
- "conformité" is not "compatibilité";
- to "suspendre" a time limit is not to "interrompre" it;
- "prescription" is not "forclusion";
- "publicité" is not "publication";
- "décision" for the Constitutional Council (*Conseil constitutionnel*), the Jurisdiction Disputes Tribunal (*Tribunal des conflits*), the Council of State and the CNDA; "arrêt" for the Court of Cassation (*Cour de cassation*), the courts of appeal, the administrative courts of appeal and the Court of Audit (*Cour des comptes*); "jugement" for the tribunals;
- the "dispositions" of a unilateral act, the "stipulations" of a contract or a treaty;
- "sur proposition" binds the author of the decision, "après avis" does not bind him;
- one cites "les dispositions de l'article x", not "l'article x";
- "précité" applies only to a text whose terms have been quoted; "visé" applies only to a text appearing in the recitals; to refer to a rule or a person mentioned elsewhere in the text, write "mentionné", "énuméré", "défini" or "institué";
- "de la présente loi", "du présent décret" is written only to remove an ambiguity between several texts cited together;
- in the body of the text, "le ministre chargé de la sécurité sociale", never the title of the minister in office, except for justice, the interior, defence and foreign affairs;
- beware of "région" and "collectivité territoriale", whose spatial scope varies, and do not designate the organ (conseil régional) for the authority (région).

### 5. Division of the text, articles and paragraphs (fiche 3.2.2)

- The article is the basic unit: one rule per article; rather several articles than one article with subdivisions.
- An article inserted between articles 23 and 24 is numbered 23-1, 23-2; between 42-2 and 42-3, 42-2-1. "Bis", "ter" only if the amended text already uses them. Do not renumber existing articles; if compelled to, correct all cross-references.
- The subdivisions I, II, III are to be avoided, especially in a code; one designates "le II de l'article 8", never "l'article 8. II".
- Roman numerals are not used to enumerate: under "L'article X est ainsi modifié :", the items are 1°, 2°, 3°, then a), b), c).
- A paragraph (*alinéa*) is everything that starts on a new line, whatever the sign that precedes it; a table constitutes a single paragraph. Avoid "l'alinéa qui précède", "l'alinéa qui suit"; refer to the rank (premier, deuxième) or, better, to the number or the letter.
- Every paragraph begins with a capital letter, except after a dash; in an enumeration, each item ends with a semicolon, the last with a full stop. Dashes are to be avoided as enumeration markers.

### 6. Punctuation (fiche 3.3.1)

- Punctuation makes the meaning: "les décrets et les autres actes pour lesquels une loi le prévoit" is not "les décrets et les autres actes, pour lesquels une loi le prévoit". Report any comma that changes the scope of a provision.
- No comma before a coordinating conjunction (mais, ou, et, donc, or, ni, car), except for a parenthetical clause; no comma after, except for a parenthetical clause.
- Each announcement of an inserted or substituted text is followed by a colon and a quotation mark: "sont remplacés par les mots : « … »".
- No full stop at the end of division headings (typographic charter, p. 700).

### 7. Numbers, dates, typography (typographic charter, p. 699 and 700)

- "Premier" and ordinals are written in full: "le premier alinéa", never "le 1er alinéa" nor "l'alinéa 1"; "les deuxième et troisième alinéas". Exception: dates and article numbers ("1er avril 2005", "article 1er").
- Sums in figures, the currency unit abbreviated without a full stop ("10 €"), multiples of a million in full ("2 milliards d'euros"); units of measurement in full in the body of the text ("100 mètres carrés"), except in tables.
- Numbers preceding a unit of measurement or a population in figures ("152 tonnes", "15 000 habitants"); durations, ages and quantities in letters ("vingt-deux mois"), except in tables.
- "%" and "‰", not "p. cent". No full stop within numbers ("1 350").
- Dates and times in figures ("24 avril 1951", "15 h 30").
- French quotation marks « », English quotation marks only for a quotation within a quotation. The headings of inserted articles are written "Art. 28. – "; Latin numberings (bis, ter) in italics.
- Capital letter on the first word of unique institutions (la Cour des comptes, le Conseil d'État); capitals are not accented in texts published in the Official Journal (*Journal officiel*), except in a text entirely in capitals.
- The names of communes, departments and local authorities take hyphens when they designate the administrative entity ("Wallis-et-Futuna"), and the article contracts ("la commune du Havre", "le préfet de Seine-et-Marne").

### 8. Recitals and "susvisé" (fiche 3.1.5)

- A bill has no recitals (*visas*).
- The texts referred to in the recitals are those that found the competence, those from which the draft derogates, those it cites and those it amends; never a text it repeals in full; never a text that merely amended another text, which is referred to with the mention "modifié" after its date.
- The recitals of an amending text are not incorporated into the amended text: an inserted provision may describe as "susvisé" only a text appearing in the recitals of the original text, and never when it is inserted in a code.
- In the recitals and the execution article, "la ministre" if the office-holder is a woman; in the body of the text, "le ministre", which designates the office (fiche 3.3.1).

### 9. Cross-references and internal consistency

- Each cross-reference ("mentionné au 2° du II") designates a subdivision that exists, under that numbering, in the text as it will result from the draft.
- The same notion keeps the same word from one end of the text to the other; a defined term is used as it is defined.
- The amendments to the same article are grouped together and ordered in the order of the amended text.

## The output

A table, one row per deviation, in the order of the text:

| passage | rule | source | proposed correction | severity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "il sera procédé à une évaluation" | present indicative, with imperative force | Legislative Drafting Guide, fiche 3.3.1 | "il est procédé à une évaluation" | deviation |

`severity` is "deviation" when the entry says so, "to be checked" when the rule rests on usage or depends on a context the proofreader does not have. End with at most two sentences: what calls for an overall revision, if any, and nothing else.

## Checks before delivering

- No deviation is reported without an entry of the Guide.
- Each passage quoted is found word for word in the text.
- Each correction is drafted, not described.
- Nothing has been said about the substance.
