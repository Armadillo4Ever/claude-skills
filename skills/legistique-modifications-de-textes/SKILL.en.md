# Legislative drafting: amendments to texts

French sources are quoted in free translation; the French text prevails.

## What this skill covers, and what it leaves to the Guide

Legislative drafting (*légistique*) is the art of designing and drafting normative texts. The Legislative Drafting Guide (*Guide de légistique*), published on Légifrance by the General Secretariat of the Government and the Council of State (*Conseil d'État*), is the authority on it. This skill takes up only one part of it, the part where errors are most frequent and least visible: **the instructions by which one text amends another**, their reading, their application to the text in force and their drafting, up to the three-column table that gives an account of them.

It does not deal with the design of the text (choice of instrument, level of norm, impact assessment), the structure of a new text (title, recitals, division into articles, execution articles), codification, or the formal proofreading of a text (punctuation, numbering, formulas, cross-references). For these questions, see the last section, which refers to the Guide part by part.

## The cardinal principle

**The operation is read from the VERB, never from the set phrase.**

"Worded as follows" (*ainsi rédigé*) is not an operator: the same phrase serves for replacement ("the first paragraph is worded as follows") and for insertion ("an article 12-1 is inserted, worded as follows"). Only the verb and its construction say what must be done. Taking "worded as follows" as a sign of replacement is the most frequent and the most costly error: it turns an insertion into an overwrite, and the consolidated text loses provisions without anything signalling it.

## Two distinct notions: the target and the object

Every instruction designates a place and puts something there.

- The **target** is the place where the operation is carried out: "chapter II", "section I", "the second paragraph", "article 7".
- The **object** is what is put there or removed from there: an article, a paragraph, a sentence, words, a subdivision.

"Chapter II is supplemented by an article 9-1" has a division as its target and an article as its object. Confusing the two leads to inserting at the wrong level. Always keep them separate.

## The vocabulary of divisions

In decreasing order: book, title, chapter, section, sub-section, paragraph, article. Within the article: the subdivisions marked I, II, III, then 1°, 2°, 3°, then a), b), c), then the paragraphs (*alinéas*, everything that starts on a new line), the sentences, the words.

Designations are **composable**, and must be resolved as such: "the last paragraph of section I", "the first sentence of the first paragraph", "sub-paragraph 4° of section II", "the third paragraph of sub-paragraph 2° of section I of article 7". Never treat a compound form as a special case: read it from right to left, from the widest container to the finest portion.

**A level runs until the next marker of the same level.** The unmarked paragraphs that follow a 33° belong to it: the 33° may have two paragraphs. Do not stop at the first line break.

**Naming the container without restricting anything means targeting the whole.** "Article L. 512-16 is worded as follows" designates no particular portion: the target is the entire article.

**"The same" is not the name of a container.** "The same second paragraph" designates a precise paragraph, not the entire text; "of the same decree" refers to the text cited previously.

## Counting paragraphs

Rule in force, stemming from the circular of 20 October 2000 on the method of counting paragraphs (NOR PRMX0004462C), which aligned government practice on parliamentary usage: **every line break opens a paragraph**, including the items of an enumeration.

Two clarifications that make the difference:

- **A table constitutes a single paragraph**, whatever the number of its rows. Counting a table row as a paragraph shifts all the following ranks, silently.
- **The modern count also applies to texts earlier than 2000.** The old count, which opened a paragraph only after a full stop, no longer has to be applied. Do not produce a warning on this point: decide, and decide modern.

## The operations and their canonical form

| Operation | Forms encountered | Target and object |
|---|---|---|
| Replace | "Article 7 is replaced by the following provisions:" (*L'article 7 est remplacé par les dispositions suivantes :*); "The first paragraph is worded as follows:" (*Le premier alinéa est ainsi rédigé :*); "the words: '...' are replaced by the words: '...'" (*les mots : « … » sont remplacés par les mots : « … »*) | variable target, object identical to the target |
| Replace the beginning | "The beginning of the first paragraph of article L. 4133-1 is worded as follows: '... (the remainder unchanged).'" (*Le début du premier alinéa de l'article L. 4133-1 est ainsi rédigé : « … (le reste sans changement). »*) | paragraph, beginning only |
| Insert after | "After article 12-3, an article 12-4 is inserted, worded as follows:" (*Après l'article 12-3, il est inséré un article 12-4 ainsi rédigé :*); "After the third paragraph of article 17, two paragraphs are inserted, worded as follows:" (*Après le troisième alinéa de l'article 17, sont insérés deux alinéas ainsi rédigés :*) | target = the element after which the insertion is made |
| Insert after words | "In the last paragraph, after the second occurrence of the words: 'section 1', the words: 'and in section 4' are inserted" (*Au dernier alinéa, après la deuxième occurrence des mots : « section 1 », sont insérés les mots : « et à la section 4 »*) | the occurrence, when specified, is part of the designation |
| Add at the end | "Chapter II is supplemented by an article 9-1, worded as follows:" (*Le chapitre II est complété par un article 9-1 ainsi rédigé :*); "At the end of chapter II, an article 9-1 is added" (*À la fin du chapitre II, est ajouté un article 9-1*) | target = the division, object = the article |
| Add at the beginning | "At the beginning of chapter III, an article 9-1 is added, worded as follows:" (*Au début du chapitre III, est ajouté un article 9-1 ainsi rédigé :*) | same, initial position |
| Add a sentence | "The third paragraph of article 12 is supplemented by a sentence worded as follows: '...'" (*Le troisième alinéa de l'article 12 est complété par une phrase ainsi rédigée : « … »*) | object = sentence, and not paragraph |
| Add words without anchor | "The words: '...' are added" (*Sont ajoutés les mots : « … »*) | the words go at the END of the subdivision considered |
| Constitute a subdivision | "The first five paragraphs constitute a section I" (*Les cinq premiers alinéas constituent un I*) | grouping, without changing the text |
| Re-enact | "An article L. 311-6 is re-enacted, worded as follows:" (*Il est rétabli un article L. 311-6 ainsi rédigé :*) | reintroduces a repealed article, in its original place |
| Renumber | "Article 14 becomes article 15" (*L'article 14 devient l'article 15*) | shift of numbering |
| Repeal | "Sub-paragraph 4° of article 5 is repealed" (*Le 4° de l'article 5 est abrogé*) | the object disappears |
| Delete | "The third paragraph of article 17 is deleted" (*Le troisième alinéa de l'article 17 est supprimé*); "the references: 'L. 343-5' are deleted" (*les références : « L. 343-5 » sont supprimées*) | deletion of a portion |

Distinguish repeal (*abrogation*), which targets a norm (article, division), from deletion (*suppression*), which targets a portion of text (paragraph, sentence, words, reference).

## Seven proven traps in reading

1. **The instruction carries its own number.** In a draft, an instruction does not begin with its verb but with its label: "6° After article...", "I. After title V...", "a) After article...". A reading rule anchored at the head of the string does not see these instructions and lets them through.
2. **The auxiliary is elided in an enumeration.** Under the announcement "Article X is amended as follows:" (*L'article X est ainsi modifié :*), the items take up the auxiliary by ellipsis: "1° Title I supplemented by articles...", "2° The second paragraph replaced by...", "3° Article 7 repealed". Requiring "is" or "are" causes all these instructions to be missed.
3. **A range designates all the intermediate elements.** "Articles 20-12 to 20-14" also covers 20-13. Do not stop at the bounds.
4. **The quotation of an insertion of several articles does not stop at the first closing quotation mark.** An intermediate article may close with a quotation mark while the quotation continues, each following paragraph reopening its own.
5. **"The remainder unchanged" (*le reste sans changement*) bounds the operation**: only the quoted portion is replaced.
6. **The occurrence counts.** "The second occurrence of the words" is not the first: apply at the right place.
7. **An article created by the text does not yet exist.** Do not look for it in the law in force and do not conclude that it cannot be found: it is instituted by the text under review.

## Applying the instruction to the text in force

Reading correctly is not enough; the instruction must also be applied at the right place.

- "After the first paragraph" inserts in second position, and not at the end.
- A sentence added to a paragraph remains in that paragraph: it does not become an autonomous paragraph.
- A table row is not a paragraph.
- A replacement of words is carried out in the designated portion, and not throughout the article.
- Check the text in its version in force **at the relevant date**, and not in its current version.

After application, reread the consolidated text in full: a correct amendment may render incoherent a cross-reference, an enumeration or a neighbouring numbering.

## Producing the three-column table

The comparative table gives an account of the amendments, article by article. Three columns, in this order:

1. **The law in force**: the amended text, in its version applicable at the date of the review, cited by its exact title and verified on Légifrance. Empty, with the mention "article created", when the draft institutes the provision.
2. **The draft**: the text of the article of the draft, reproduced in full and as it stands, without rewording or summary.
3. **The consolidated text**: what the law in force will become if the draft is adopted, deleted passages struck through and added passages marked. This column is **always filled**, including for a self-standing provision that amends no text: it then reproduces its text, since that text will be the law.

One row per article or amended subdivision, in the order of the draft. An instruction that cannot be applied, because its target could not be identified, is reported as such in column 3, never applied by guesswork nor passed over in silence.

## Drafting an amendment

- Designate the target from the widest to the finest, without ambiguity, and cite the amended text by its full title the first time.
- Use the verb that says exactly the operation, and one operation per instruction.
- Quote in quotation marks the words inserted or replaced, without altering them.
- Prefer the complete rewriting of an article to an accumulation of micro-amendments that make the text illegible.
- Draw the consequences: coordinations, cross-references, consequential repeals, transitional provisions.

## Entry into force and transitional provisions

Systematically check: the date of entry into force, the fate of ongoing situations, the necessary implementing measures and their time limit, the articulation with repealed texts, and, where relevant, application in the overseas territories.

## What this skill leaves to the Legislative Drafting Guide

The Legislative Drafting Guide can be consulted online on Légifrance. For everything that follows, refer to it rather than improvising, citing the entry (*fiche*) consulted:

- **Design of texts**: necessity and expediency of the text, choice of legal instrument, allocation between statute and regulation, impact assessment, application in time.
- **Stages of preparation**: mandatory consultations, review by the Council of State, signature, countersignature, publication.
- **Drafting of texts**: title, recitals, division of the text, established formulas, punctuation and typography, abbreviations, cross-references, definitions, transitional and final provisions, overseas territories.
- **Rules specific to certain categories**: ordinances, decrees adopted after consultation of the Council of State, ministerial or prefectoral orders, transposition texts, codification.

The formal proofreading of a text (punctuation, numbering, formulas, cross-references) is a matter for a distinct legislative proofreading skill; the legal review of a draft is a matter for the advisory review skill.

## Checks before delivering

- The operation adopted corresponds to the verb, not to the set phrase.
- The target and the object are distinguished and named.
- The rank of the paragraphs has been calculated according to the modern count, tables counted as one.
- The compound designations have been fully resolved.
- Every article cited has been verified in its applicable version, and the articles created by the text are flagged as such.
- The consolidated text has been reread in full, and column 3 of the table is filled on every row.
